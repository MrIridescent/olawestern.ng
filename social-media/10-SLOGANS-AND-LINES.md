## 3. Words that do the work, and words that cost you

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| financed, built, drilled, cleared, submitted, secured, negotiated, delivered, published | transformed, revolutionised, solved, champion, powerhouse | Verbs with receipts vs adjectives with none |
| "we pushed for and the State approved" | "we built it" | Precision is legally and reputationally safer, and it is true |
| "a standing request" | "a done deal" | Candour buys credibility for everything else |
| "your street, your avenue, your gate" | "the good people of Amuwo-Odofin" | Second person singular beats collective flattery |
| "if elected" | "when I deliver" (pre-office) | Forward claims must be labelled as plans (`00` §5) |
| "ask them for their source" | "they are liars" | The dare survives scrutiny; the accusation invites a suit |

## 4. Pidgin and Yoruba — how to use them without embarrassing the campaign

The campaign's own brief commits to English, Nigerian Pidgin and Yoruba. The rule: **Pidgin by a speaker, Yoruba by a native speaker, always.** A mis-turned Yoruba proverb in a state-house race is a gift to every rival — and a mistranslated Pidgin line reads as an outsider pretending.

**Process (mandatory before any non-English asset goes out):**
1. Draft in English.
2. Convert by a named team member who speaks it daily.
3. Read it aloud to two people from the target ward. If they wince, rewrite.
4. Log the reviewer's name and date next to the line.
5. Yoruba orthography and tone marks get a native-speaker check specifically — the campaign has no Yoruba copy cleared yet.

**Pidgin examples — marked as examples for the team to adapt, not approved copy:**
- `Na so we do am — we start before election, we no go stop after.` (frame: the dated record)
- `Come look the record make you judge.` (frame: T-22)
- `We no dey collect money from anybody. If anybody claim say na for us, send am come.` (frame: T-36)
- `Your vote na your voice. No anybody fit take am from you.` (frame: polling day, service tone)
- `Which road dem do? Which one dem promise? Make we compare.` (frame: contrast, pressure)

**Yoruba:** none is cleared in this file. Two instructions instead: (a) have the ward's Yoruba-speaking elders suggest a line of their own — an idiom the community authored travels further than one the campaign invented; (b) never attribute a proverb to a person or an institution you have not verified, and never place a religious formula in a political mouth.

## 6. Jingle and audio (the street layer)

Keep it to 12–18 seconds; anything longer gets skipped on a phone and shouted over by a generator.

Structure: **call → answer → name → slogan → date.**
```
[call, one voice, unhurried]  Amuwo-Odofin!
[answer, crowd, recorded at a real event with consent]  Ha!
[verse, 4 lines max, one claim each]
   Boreholes for six wards, we don do am
   7th Avenue drainage, we carry am
   Six roads for FESTAC, dem approve am
   Record open — come check am
[lock]  Ola Western! Towards responsible representation!
[service]  Sixteen, O January. APC. House of Assembly, Amuwo Odofin One.
```
Every claim in that verse maps to `00-CLAIMS-LEDGER.md` R-1, R-2, R-4. **Do not add a verse about health funds, tournaments or vigilantes** — those lines are HOLD, and a jingle is the hardest asset to recall. Record the crowd answer at your own event with announced consent; never a studio foley crowd pretending to be a real one (`03` §7). Noise permits and the 24-hour pre-poll silence apply: `05` §6 and `08` §2.

# Street marketing and ground game — Amuwo-Odofin Constituency I

**Subject:** Hon. Engr. Folorunsho Olaitan Segun ("Ola Western"), APC candidate, Lagos State House of Assembly, Amuwo-Odofin Constituency I. **HQ:** 32 Road by 3rd Avenue, FESTAC Town. **Slogan:** "... towards responsible representation".
**Day 1 = Tuesday 3 November 2026. Poll = Saturday 16 January 2027, 74 days later.** Plan runs Day 1–60 (to Friday 1 January 2027), then hands into a two-week get-out-the-vote push, Day 61–74.
**Wards:** Festac I (F-I, HQ), Festac II (F-II), Festac III (F-III), Mile 2 / Housing Estate (M2). **Languages:** English, Pidgin, Yoruba — knock in the resident's own language. Digital is a sibling file; the only digital instruction here is §5.5.

## 1. Strategy in five lines

1. A state House race turns on three things in one constituency: ward-level name recognition, agent quality at the unit level, and whether supporters already identified actually arrive on 16 January.
2. Messaging does not move it. The few people a voter has personally met — captain, unit agent, the two knockers at the gate — do.
3. Street and content are one loop: every action yields something shootable, published the same evening. A street day with no photographs is half done.
4. If you cannot attach a report, document or dated photo set, it is a plan and is said as a plan, never as a fact.
5. Two hours with one estate beats forty minutes with ten. The ladder and agent training must exist before Day 45; training cannot start in the freeze week.

## 4. Door-to-door

**4.1 Ninety-second script, English.** "Good afternoon. My name is [[NAME]], your neighbour on [[STREET]]. We are going door to door in [[WARD]] for Ola Western — Engr. Folorunsho Segun, APC candidate for Lagos House of Assembly in Amuwo-Odofin One. He is a certified civil engineer and runs Western Concrete in FESTAC Town. Two things done without anyone asking: boreholes in six wards in 2018, and the 7th Avenue drains desilted the same year. Now the state has approved six roads on the assessments he presented, 1st Avenue first. The record is on this card. I am not asking for your vote today — three questions, then I go."

**4.2 Pidgin variant — an example to adapt, not to read stiffly.** "Good afternoon. My name na [[NAME]], I na your neighbour for [[STREET]]. We dey go house to house for [[WARD]] for Ola Western — Engr. Folorunsho Segun, APC candidate for Amuwo-Odofin One, State House of Assembly. Na engineer, him own Western Concrete for inside FESTAC. Watin we do without anybody beg am: borehole for six wards for 2018, and we clean 7th Avenue drain for same year. Now, government don approve road six wey him assessment carry — 1st Avenue na first. Everything dey inside this card. I no dey beg your vote today. Question small tin make I ask you, then I go."

**4.3 Three questions, every door, in this order.** 1) "Who in this house will vote in January?" 2) "What is the one thing on this street you want your assembly member to fix first?" — recorded verbatim. 3) "May we note you down for one reminder before 16 January?" — consent, §3.

**4.4 Record, not promise.** Ledger items are said as facts, with the document named. Anything outside §2 is said as "that is a plan: [[STATE THE PROJECT, DATE AND SOURCE]]". The four HOLD lines are not said at all, by anyone, in any form. Nothing about any other candidate is said on a knock; if a resident raises an allegation about an opponent, the reply is "we are not going to talk about that today", and the agent logs the mood, not the rumour.

**4.5 Leave-behind card.** A6 (105 × 148 mm) or 90 × 50 mm, 300 gsm matte. Front: photo, Ola Western, APC symbol, "Candidate, Lagos State House of Assembly, Amuwo-Odofin Constituency I", **that ward's name**, slogan. Back: WhatsApp [[NUMBER]], QR [[LINK]], three ledger claims, that ward's standing issues, HQ address. Never print a HOLD line, an unproved promise or an opponent's name.

**4.6 Hostile resident.** Do not answer back. "You are right to push back. What exactly do you want me to write down?" Write it in front of them, read it back, hand over the card, thank them, leave inside sixty seconds. Log `opposition — reason recorded`. Never argue at a gate, never with a phone pointed at you, and do not return to that street that week.

**4.7 No cash.** No money, food, fare, airtime or "small something" changes hands at any door, ever, by anyone. That is the line between canvassing and a criminal offence (§9.4), and it protects the volunteer on the step. If a resident offers cash to attend something, record it and report it the same day.

**4.8 Tally fields.** Date · ward · street · pair initials · doors knocked · answered · promised Y/N · undecided · refused · opposition reason verbatim · issue verbatim · consent Y/N · name and phone if consent · PU code · card left Y/N · route-sheet photo Y/N.

## 5. Market and street blitz

**5.1 When, route, who speaks.** Markets move before 09:00 and thin by 11:00: enter M2 **06:20**, out by **08:10**; Agboju **06:40–08:30**; no blitz on a Sunday. One fixed spine, stall by stall, exiting nearest the bus stop so we never cut against the flow. The coordinator opens; the candidate speaks to traders in their own language; one trader representative gets **more time than the candidate**; two note-takers walk behind; the media pair shoots without blocking a customer.

**5.2 Fixed twenty minutes.** 0–2 arrive, greet the association leader by name, agree which rows we may walk · 2–6 candidate: one ledger fact, one ward issue, no promises · 6–13 traders speak uninterrupted, notes read back aloud · 13–16 **one** question answered fully, the rest parked with a named date · 16–18 card and WhatsApp join, nothing handed over · 18–20 photograph the route sheet, sweep litter we did not make, leave.

**5.3 Complaints, without leaving the remit.** Write it down, name the body that owns it, say what this office can and cannot do. Waste and street-trading enforcement: LAWMA and the LGA — oversight and pressure, not bin removal. FHA ground rent and tenure (F-III): federal authority — he can escalate, convene and pursue state-side relief, not sign a deed. Mile 2 jetty and trailer-bridge: federal and port-adjacent; the evidenced move is the NIWA agreement that wrote Mile 2 youths into operation, maintenance and security. FESTAC roads and drainage: state assets, answered by the approved six-road rehabilitation headlined by 1st Avenue. Use: **"That is real, here is who owns it, and here is what this office can and cannot do."**

**5.4 Safety and dignity.** No blocking stalls, no turning a trader from a customer, no loudspeaker inside a shop, no stall interiors, no photographing a face without a verbal yes, no child as a prop. One steward per ten people, armband on, briefed only to stop our own crowd crowding anyone. Leave the place cleaner than we found it, carrying our own bags out. Disperse M2 by 08:30. Permissions or procession requirements on association ground **[CONFIRM WITH A LAWYER / LGA PERMIT OFFICE]**.

**5.5 Content loop.** The media pair hands the raw set to the desk before leaving; the lead publishes that evening — route sheet, the trader's own words, date and row named, ledger claim cited to source. Minimum three pieces per street day.

## 8. Faith, labour and community doors

**Requesting an audience.** Write to the bodies already in the record — CAN Amuwo-Odofin chapter and the Chief Imam of Ifelodun Central Mosque appear in attested Food Fest reporting — referencing that shared work and asking for a date and a slot. Never a drop-in with a camera crew. Same letter to churches, trade unions and labour bodies, LAWMA waste-worker associations, market-women leaders, the PWD community contact, tenant and ratepayers' groups, age grades and town unions: date, the ask, the record, and one issue we want their advice on.

**Appropriate:** sitting, listening, noting, bringing the engineer's facts, leaving without asking for a blessing, a pulpit minute or a public endorsement. **Avoid:** any appearance that turns a place of worship into a campaign venue; asking to announce a candidacy during a service; money, materials or contractor work offered to a body's leadership. We do not sort, brief or route supporters by religion or ethnic origin and say nothing about any community "as a bloc" — law and safety, and Mile 2's multi-ethnic character is a reason for ordinary neutrality, not messaging. If a leader invites him to address a congregation, the content is the record and the ARBR pledge, plus [[ANY ADDITIONAL ITEM THE BODY APPROVES IN WRITING]].

## 9. Pre-poll and polling day

**9.1 Unit-agent training** — one three-hour session per ward, finished by Day 45.

- [ ] Knows PU code, address, feeder lanes; walked them twice
- [ ] Holds accreditation; if refused entry, stands outside and calls the coordinator — escalation route **[CONFIRM]**
- [ ] Knows the order: open · vote · count · EC8A posted · sealed
- [ ] Trained on photographing and sending the posted sheet (§9.3)
- [ ] Trained on the tally form and the 30-minute submission clock
- [ ] Conflict rules: no argument, no crowd at the entrance, nothing touched, nothing signed under pressure — write, photograph, report
- [ ] Phone charged with data; paper register as backup; no-cash and no-transport rule restated (§4.7, §9.4)
- [ ] Signs the code-of-conduct and data-handling acknowledgement; two named people can reach them

**9.2 Arrival.** Agent on site before the queue forms. In the pre-poll week the desk visits every PU with its agent and photographs the location so supporters can find the gate. Statutory timing, entry and material-handling rules **[CONFIRM WITH A LAWYER / LGA PERMIT OFFICE]**, against current INEC regulations and the Electoral Act 2022 (as amended).

**9.3 Photograph the results sheet, lawfully.** The war-room design already assumes agents uploading photos of the posted result sheet for digitisation and cross-check against ground data.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Photograph the sheet **as publicly posted** — counts and signatures, after the count is displayed | Photograph ballots, boxes, BVAS or card readers, or officials' materials |
| Photograph your own tally sheet and the queue from where the public stands | Photograph inside the booth, other agents' documents, voters' PVCs or faces |
| Keep the file unedited, time and place attached; submit to the ward desk within 30 minutes | Crop, brighten or re-order a result image; publish before the desk cross-checks |
| Whether photography at a unit is permitted, and what may be kept: **[CONFIRM WITH A LAWYER / LGA PERMIT OFFICE]** | Film a confrontation to provoke a reaction |

**9.4 Transport, and the line nobody crosses.** Transport or money given to influence attendance or voting is a corrupt practice and a criminal offence: this campaign does not organise, fund, promise or announce voter transport, does not give fare, and keeps no list of people carried. A supporter who independently drives a neighbour, unasked and unreimbursed, is a different act — but it sits near the line, so nothing announced, nothing listed, nothing reimbursed, and written advice taken before anyone organises lifting for elderly or disabled supporters **[CONFIRM WITH A LAWYER]**. What we do instead is legal and useful: publish each PU's address and walking directions for every street, on the card and in the same-evening content, so nobody needs a favour to find their gate.

**9.5 War-room tally loop**, on the existing runbook rhythm. Agent photo plus five numbers → ward desk within 30 minutes → tally clerk keys it, a second person re-keys the same sheet → mismatch to the coordinator at once → desk pushes the row to the war room → checks at 08:00 and 20:00 through the campaign period, rolling on polling day → only the communications lead publishes, and no result is commented on publicly before cross-check. Two-person coverage across waking hours; content changes only, no deploys, from Day 68.

## 13. Refusals: eight things this campaign does not do on the street

| We do not | Because |
|---|---|
| Pay, feed, transport or give airtime to anyone to attend | **Law** — a corrupt practice and criminal offence; a paid crowd teaches the street our numbers are rented |
| Bus in outsiders and present them as spontaneous support | **Law, reputation** — fake support is detectable and permanently converts neutral voters against us |
| Use thugs, "boys" or age-grade muscle, or any show of force | **Law, safety** — violence and criminal exposure; it frightens the very turnout we need |
| Discourage, obstruct or "manage" voters or rival agents, or seed doubt that the vote will happen | **Law** — suppression is criminal and self-defeating in a four-ward race where everyone learns who did it |
| Say anything about opponents that is not in our own evidenced file | **Fabrication** — it hands them the story, and our only advantage is that we attach a document |
| Paint walls or place signage without written permission; block traffic, junctions or market flow | **Law, safety** — unlawful use of someone's property and a public risk; it makes us the reason the street was stuck |
| Offer money, contracts, jobs or contractor favouritism to officials or association leaders | **Law** — bribery, and here it turns the gatekeepers we need against us |
| Ask anyone to speak, sit or vote along ethnic or religious lines, or use a worship venue as a platform | **Law, safety** — incitement is criminal, the wards are mixed, and it endangers the people who live there |

And the quiet ninth, the rule the campaign's public record already runs on: **we do not say a thing we cannot attach a document to.**

## 2. The one rule that makes all of this work

**Every post states a fact the campaign can prove, or it states a plan and calls it a plan.**

That is the same rule the website's publish gate enforces (`../ola-western-web/docs/00-DOCUMENT-INDEX.md`: no track-record entry or Truth Room ruling is created without its evidence link). Social is where the rule gets broken — a volunteer typing fast at midnight is the leak, not the code. So:

1. Open `00-CLAIMS-LEDGER.md`, find the fact, use its exact wording and number.
2. If the fact isn't in the ledger, it is a **plan** ("we will push for…") or a **slot** you fill from a document, or it does not go out.
3. Four claims the campaign would most like to make are currently **HOLD** — an indigent hospital-bills relief fund, personal sponsorship of the Penalty King youth tournament, funding of vigilante patrol logistics, and "he lives and worships inside FESTAC Town". They are plausible and they are in the campaign's own profile document, but no published report in the project's 21 press references supports them. The site's self-audit already grades them `block`. Posting them on Facebook while the website refuses to publish them is the fastest way for one journalist, one opponent, or one curious WhatsApp commenter to make the whole record look invented. Get the document — a receipt, an award certificate, a newspaper page, a photograph with a dated caption — attach it, then use them.
4. A human approves every post. Nothing scheduled, nothing "AI-generated" goes out unapproved. This matches the campaign's own stated policy that the AI never posts independently.

## 3. What you asked for, and what I built instead of it

You asked for "pysops, dark psychology in the text". Here is the honest split, and it is a professional judgement rather than a refusal for its own sake:

**Delivered, in full:** aggressive, ethical persuasion. Identity framing, loss framing, concrete-imagery writing, name repetition and mere exposure, real social proof, authority transfer from attested endorsements, commitment-and-consistency ladders, urgency from real dates, planning-interruption questions, contrast against the record of the office, reciprocity through service, curiosity-gap hooks, and rhythm-level sentence craft that makes a paragraph feel inevitable. See `03-PERSUASION-PLAYBOOK.md` and the hooks built into every template. Most of what people call "dark" in political copy is just knowing how a reader's attention works, and that is in here.

**Not delivered:** the deception half. Specifically: fake grassroots and sockpuppet accounts, astroturfed comment sections, bought followers or engagement, fabricated rumours about opponents, invented "sources", fear content about violence or attacks that nobody has attested, ethnic or religious incitement, content designed to make a group think polling is rigged in order to keep them home, impersonation pages, undisclosed paid political reach dressed as organic, and AI-generated images or voice of the candidate. Not because the campaign lacks the nerve, but because each one is either a criminal/civil exposure under Nigerian law and platform policy, or a bomb with a delay fuse: it wins a week and detonates in front of the 21 press references you are standing on. In a 74-day race, a single caught fabrication costs more than any honest post can gain. There is also a practical point — this candidate's entire differentiated position is *"his record is open for inspection"*. That line only works if the campaign is the one party in the race that cannot be caught lying.

If you want the maximum legal intensity, it is in `03` §6: the "pressure" section (real scarcity, real deadlines, name-and-shame of documented inaction, contrast framing, repeated unanswered questions to an incumbent's record). That is where the leverage actually is.

## 4. How to run this folder in practice

- **Who posts:** one named page administrator, two named backups. Nobody else has the password; volunteers get templates, not access.
- **Cadence:** 2 posts per day on the Facebook page minimum (see `06` §2), 1 video per day from `04`'s slate, 3 street actions per week from `05`, all tracked in `07`.
- **Approval:** the media aide drafts → the ward coordinator confirms any local fact → the campaign secretary approves → post. Total time target: 20 minutes, which is why templates exist.
- **Storage:** every published post goes in a dated log with its ledger fact and screenshot. When someone later claims the campaign said something it didn't, the log is the defence.
- **The 15-minute rule:** the intake strategy documents set a 15-minute response protocol for emerging rumours. `09` §5 is built around it.
- **Never from a personal account.** Everything publishes from the official page; the handles cleared for use are listed in `06` §1.

# 03 — Persuasion playbook

**Compiled:** 3 November 2026
**What this file is:** how a reader's attention and judgement actually work, applied to a Lagos state-house race, inside the line the campaign cannot cross without destroying the one asset it has — being the only actor in the race whose claims come with documents.

You asked for "pysops, dark psychology". Section 6 is the strongest legitimate pressure available to you and it is genuinely uncomfortable to run. Section 7 is the manipulation toolkit, listed so you can see exactly what is being refused and why refusing it is the better tactic. Read §7 last; it is short.

---

## 1. The seven principles doing the work (and how each looks in a post)

**Reciprocity — give something real first.** Boreholes in 2018, drainage in 2018, food in 2024, a technical brief that won approvals. The rhetorical form: *"he did it years before anybody needed a vote"* (T-23). Reciprocity is only persuasion when the gift is unprompted; when it arrives eight days before the ballot it reads as rent, and everyone knows it. Which is why the campaign's dated record — not its December packages — is the asset.

**Social proof — but true.** A crowd of people you paid is a liability that a single photograph can expose. Real social proof: 12 of 20 delegates in 2022; 3,000+ votes in 2026; over twenty published reports; a notebook with [[N]] issues written in it (T-30). Also: "eleven households on this avenue have already said they're going" — say it only if it's true and can be shown.

**Authority — transferred, never invented.** An engineer's title, a construction company that employs people locally, the Governor's office approving a brief, the Chief Imam and CAN supervising a distribution, ARBR's forum. Authority travels through documents and institutions, not through adjectives. Never "unanimous" unless a document says unanimous.

**Consistency — make small public yeses.** The ladder: react to a post → comment your ward → send "JOIN [[WARD]]" on WhatsApp → attend a street table → collect a card for a neighbour → agent on polling day. Each step is a public act the person will want to remain consistent with. Ask for one step at a time (T-33, T-34).

**Liking — through similarity, not flattery.** Name their street. Use the word "bail", "NEPA", "danfo", "FHA", "the canal", "generator". Persuasion fails when the copy sounds written in an office in another LGA.

**Scarcity — real deadlines only.** 16 January 2027 is a scarce resource with a fixed date. Never invent one ("last chance", "tomorrow we close the list") unless it's literally true; a caught fake deadline poisons every future urgent post.

**Unity — "one of us".** The single most effective frame available here, and it is fully evidenced: his company is in FESTAC Town and his campaign office is at 32 Road by 3rd Avenue, two streets away. That is the claim (T-02). Do not extend it into residence or worship (HOLD H-4) — the address already does the work.

## 2. Line-level craft — what makes a paragraph feel inevitable

1. **Line 1 is the whole advertisement.** Facebook shows roughly three lines before "See more" on mobile. If the point is in line 4, it does not exist. Put the fact, the street or the question first.
2. **Short first sentence.** Six words or fewer lands as authority. *"Twenty years. Read that again."*
3. **Concrete nouns beat adjectives.** "Rice, yams, beans, garri, condiments, live poultry" persuades more than "generous empowerment package" — because the reader can see it, and because specificity implies verification.
4. **One number per paragraph.** Two numbers force the reader to do arithmetic and they stop reading.
5. **Second person singular.** "You", "your street", "your children's school fees" — never "the good people of Amuwo-Odofin", which is how people describe voters they have not met.
6. **Verbs the record can survive.** He *financed*, *built*, *submitted*, *secured*, *negotiated*, *delivered*. Avoid *transformed*, *revolutionised*, *solved* — those words require proof nobody asks for and instantly lower trust in the rest of the sentence.
7. **Rhythm by rule of three, then break it.** Two items plus one unexpected one is the classic political cadence: *"Elderly residents. Widows. Young people without work."*
8. **End with a single ask.** Two asks means neither gets done. Share, or comment, or WhatsApp — pick one.
9. **Sentence length variation is the "voice".** Three long sentences and then a four-word one. Read every post aloud before it goes; if you run out of breath, so does the reader.
10. **Repetition is the message.** Repeat the name and the slogan in the same form every time. Recognition compounds and is called mere exposure; it is also why the misspelled tags in `01` §3 cost money.

## 3. Framing — same fact, different effect

| Neutral frame | Stronger frame (still true) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "He built boreholes." | "Six wards got water from a FESTAC company that was asked to help — and did." | Attribution to local capacity, not charity |
| "Flooding is bad." | "The canal is not the problem. Silt and nobody owning the maintenance is." | Mechanism signals competence; complaint signals nothing |
| "He secured road approval." | "He wrote calculations and put them in front of the Governor. Six roads were approved. 1st Avenue was headlined." | Process persuades more than outcome when the outcome is government's |
| "Rumours are false." | "Here is the fact and here is the paper. Ask whoever forwarded it for their source." | Shifts the burden of proof onto the rumour |
| "Vote for us." | "Compare the answers, not the jingles." | Invites judgement rather than asking for loyalty |
| "23 Road isn't done." | "It is a standing request we are pushing — anyone claiming it is finished is selling you a story." | Candour as differentiation (T-15) |

**Loss framing where it fits.** People act harder to avoid losing something than to gain an equal benefit — and in this constituency the honest loss frame is available: *"Twenty years of facelifts that arrive and stop"* (T-21), *"Which shops close early because the avenue is dark"* (T-16). Use loss framing only about real, dated, verifiable conditions.

**Anchor with a number you can prove.** Open a record post with the strongest figure: *"Over 1,000 households."* Everything after it is measured against a number you can source.

## 4. Trust architecture — five devices that make a campaign look credible

1. **Publish your own standard.** "Nothing goes out about our candidate without a document — and nothing goes out about anybody else either" (T-38). A stated rule that binds you too is the cheapest credibility in politics.
2. **Invite accountability.** "Hold him to it. That is the point of writing it down" (T-12). Voluntary vulnerability reads as strength.
3. **Volunteer a limitation.** "We are not pretending a problem is finished" (T-06). Naming one gap makes eleven claims believable.
4. **Refuse money publicly.** T-36 ("nobody is collecting support from you") converts the default suspicion of politics into a differentiator.
5. **Keep a public record.** The website's press page, the notebook photographs, the quarterly publication promise (T-24). Persistence is proof; a page with 60 dated posts out-argues a rival with 3 polished ones.

## 6. The legitimate pressure playbook — what "hard" actually looks like here

**6.1 The dated dare.** Publish your own record with dates, then invite comparison (T-13, T-22). It is aggressive, it is fully lawful, and it only works if the receipts are attached.

**6.2 Mechanism naming.** Describe exactly why a failure persists — silt, jurisdiction, FHA-council-state ambiguity, unmaintained assets (T-14, T-19). Naming the mechanism accuses the system without accusing a person, which keeps you out of defamation trouble and makes you look like the only adult reading the file.

**6.3 The published standard.** Promise something gradeable and quarterly (T-24). Every rival's vagueness then becomes their problem rather than yours.

**6.4 Question repetition.** One unanswered question, repeated in different forms over a fortnight, is worth twenty angry posts. *"What happened to the money allocated for [[X]]?"* — only where a real allocation or report exists.

**6.5 Prebuttal (inoculation).** Warn supporters the rumour is coming before it arrives, and hand them the check to run: *"by evening you will see… ask for the source"* (T-37, T-38). Attitude-inoculation research is on your side here: naming the attack technique makes people resistant to it, including your own supporters when an opponent uses it on them.

**6.6 Silence as pressure.** When a rival's claim is baseless and you have proof, one post with the document attached and no adjectives is more damaging than a rebuttal war. Then log the claim and let the record sit.

**6.7 The refusal as theatre.** Publishing what you will not do — no money collected, no fake account, no fabricated story — is pressure applied to the field, because it forces everyone else to answer whether they do those things.

## 7. What is refused, and the strategic reason

Each of these is a technique that works in the short run. Each is excluded because of what it costs this specific campaign — whose entire differentiated position is *"his record is open for inspection"* (T-01). One caught fabrication converts that line into evidence of the opposite.

| Technique | Why excluded |
|---|---|
| Astroturfing: sockpuppet accounts, fake grassroots groups, a staffed comment section pretending to be spontaneous | Detectable by platform integrity teams, and embarrassingly so when a neighbour recognises the same four names; it also removes the only comment data you can trust |
| Bought followers and engagement pods | Inflates a number nobody votes on, and every real engagement rate drop afterward makes the page look dead |
| Fabricated rumours or "exposé" content about opponents, including recycled unverified voice notes | Nigerian defamation and cyber-speech exposure, platform removal risk, and it hands the rival your verification standard as their weapon |
| Fear content about violence, ethnic or religious threat that nobody has attested | Incitement risk, real-world safety of your own street captains, and Mile 2 is multi-ethnic by design — you would be campaigning against your own voters |
| Anything designed to make a group believe the poll is rigged so they stay home | That is vote suppression; unlawful, and if it becomes a pattern attributed to the campaign it can reach the election result itself |
| Synthetic media: AI video, AI voice, or AI photographs of a real person — the candidate or anyone else | Explicitly refused in the project's own guardrails; EU AI Act transparency rules on synthetic content became applicable 2 August 2026, platforms treat undeclared synthetic political media as removal-grade, and one synthetic frame discovered in a campaign's own archive ends the "record open for inspection" story permanently |
| Undisclosed paid reach presented as organic enthusiasm, or buying ads in ways the platform's political-ads rules require you to declare and you don't | Ad-account suspension mid-race is the single most expensive operational failure available to you (`08` §5) |
| Impersonating or naming-riding rival accounts and candidates' hashtags | Petty, traceable, and it forfeits the meta-position |
| Cash, transport or goods conditioned on attendance or vote | A criminal offence under Nigerian electoral law, and it teaches the street that your support is rented — which is the exact belief the campaign exists to break |
| Covert filming of residents, or publishing a face without consent | NDPA exposure plus a real community grievance; `04` §3 has the consent form instead |
| "Dark triad" personality framing of a rival (insinuating character defects) | Unprovable claims about a person's character are the fastest route to a defamation letter and the slowest route to a swing voter |

**And the honest reason, not just the risk reason:** every item above is a loan taken against the campaign's credibility at a predatory interest rate. Sixty-plus days is not long enough to repay it, and the lender collects on television.

## 8. Testing and iteration

- Post one idea, not three. You cannot learn anything from a muddled post.
- Two formats per week run head-to-head on the same idea (photo vs text-only; question vs statement) and keep the winner as the template.
- Track per post: reach, comments from people in the four wards, WhatsApp joiners, shares. **Shares and ward-comments are the two numbers that predict a vote; likes predict nothing.**
- Kill a format after three attempts if its share rate sits below the page's median. `06` §7 has the weekly review sheet.
- Never delete a post to hide a bad number — screenshot it, learn, and note it. The audit trail is the campaign's habit.

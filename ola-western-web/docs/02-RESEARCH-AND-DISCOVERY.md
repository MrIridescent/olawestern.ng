## 1. Where the brief came from

This was not a greenfield product exercise. The campaign supplied a body of planning material before any code was written, and the site inherits its priorities from that material. The inputs, as received:

| Input | What it established | Effect on the product |
|---|---|---|
| A candidate profile and political-trajectory document | Who the candidate is, what offices and service work are attested, and which personal details are undocumented | `/about` shows only confirmed rows; undocumented fields stay `null` and off the site |
| A comprehensive digital-campaign-strategy and "AI War Room" deployment document | The campaign wanted monitoring, verification and rapid response as a system, not a website | The Truth Engine, `POST /api/truth-room/sync`, and the CMS's role as the war room's publishing arm |
| A "four strategic pathways" document for the constituency | Four legislative programme commitments | `/blueprint` — four deliverables, one per pathway |
| A pre-and-post-flagbearer narrative document | The candidate's public standing changed after emerging as the party's nominee | The `/impact` framing — "Before He Sought Office" — deliberately separates attested service work from campaign promises |
| An end-to-end technical manual and developer-workflow document for the war room | A vendor-side expectation that documentation, not just software, is the deliverable | This entire document set |
| An outreach-and-pitch document for campaign leadership | The engagement is also a commercial one; the vendor is seeking repeat work | `career-pack/` and the anonymisation convention |
| Direct instructions from the client, recorded verbatim in the PRD §15 | Mobile-first, calm colours, no stubs or placeholders, "PR and reputation management" purpose, YouTube embeds, image harvesting with dimension matching, vendor footer attribution | The P0/P1 requirement ordering |

The most consequential thing in that list is not any single instruction. It is that the campaign had already decided what the site was *for*: reputation management and public relations. A reputation asset is judged by the worst sentence in it, not the average, which is why the publish gate (ADR-008) became the organising principle instead of a validation detail.

## 2. Environment research — who will actually use this

**The device assumption.** Nigeria's internet access is overwhelmingly mobile, and the live network mix (2G/3G/4G/5G subscription shares and teledensity) is published by the Nigerian Communications Commission in its industry statistics: https://ncc.gov.ng/market-data-reports/industry-statistics. Precise constituency-level penetration could not be obtained — no public dataset reaches Amuwo-Odofin — so the project uses the national mobile-share pattern as its proxy and states the substitution. **[verified as a source; not verified as a constituency figure]**

**The affordability assumption.** The Alliance for Affordable Internet "1 for 2" target — 1 GB of mobile data for no more than 2% of average monthly income, a UN Broadband Commission target since 2018 — is documented here: https://adi.a4ai.org/affordable-internet-journey-from-1-to-5/. Nigeria has historically sat above that threshold. The design consequence is direct and was applied throughout: a page must not spend the user's data before they ask it to. Hence the click-to-play video facade (ADR-021), no third-party payload before a tap, and the four-runtime-dependency ceiling (ADR-028).

**The page-weight comparison.** HTTP Archive's Web Almanac gives a mobile median total page weight of 2,311 KB with 558 KB of JavaScript (2024 edition, published 30 Dec 2024: https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/page-weight and https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/javascript) and roughly 2,559 KB / 646 KB JS on 2025 data (published 15 Jan 2026: https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight). **Important caveat, and it applies to how this project cites itself:** those medians are global and no developing-market split is published. This site's first load is 87–102 kB of shared/page JS against those medians, which is a real and favourable comparison of the *JavaScript* budget only — not a claim that the whole page is 100 KB when 13 MB of photography sits in `public/`.

**The lab-conditions assumption.** Lighthouse emulates approximately the 85th-percentile mobile connection — 150 ms RTT, 1.6 Mbps down / 0.75 Mbps up, with 4× CPU throttling: https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/blob/main/docs/throttling.md. That is a reasonable but unmeasured proxy for a Festac phone, and the project records it as such: no field measurement over a real Nigerian mobile network has been taken (PRD NFR-8, and a release-blocking item).

## 3. Standards research

### 3.1 Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation of 5 October 2023, reissued as an Updated Recommendation on 12 December 2024 (errata only) — https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ and the "what's new" summary https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/. It adds nine success criteria and removes 4.1.1 Parsing. The ones that bear on this product:

- **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), AA — 24×24 CSS px.** The product's working minimum is 44–48 px, above the floor, because a 24 px target in direct sunlight on a 5-inch phone is technically compliant and practically frustrating.
- **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (A)** — inherited from 2.1 but decisive here, because the site has an auto-advancing carousel and an animated stat strip. The Understanding document (updated 10 Aug 2026) is explicit that auto-updating content must be pausable, stoppable and hideable unless essential: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html. **Status: partially met and worth naming.** The carousel is user-advanceable and pausable by touch-drag, and `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured, but there is no explicit visible pause control and no stop control for the stat ticker. This is the single clearest accessibility gap the standard research exposes, and it was not known when the carousel was built.
- **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA** — relevant because the site has a sticky header and a sticky thumb-reach bar, both of which can obscure the focused element. `scroll-margin-top` is applied for anchor targets; the keyboard-focus case is untested.
- **3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A)** and **3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (AA)** — both touch the CMS and the capture forms. Accessible Authentication matters for a desk that may include people who cannot reliably transcribe a CAPTCHA; this system's auth is a password with no cognitive test, which satisfies the spirit and probably the letter.

**The Nigerian legal position, which the project should not overstate.** There is no binding web-accessibility statute in Nigeria reaching a private or political website. The closest instruments are the Discrimination Against Persons with Disabilities (Prohibition) Act 2018 and the NCPWD Accessibility Regulations 2023 made under its s.56, whose implementation is still nascent (https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/wp-content/uploads/sites/15/2019/11/Nigeria_Discrimination-Against-Persons-with-Disabilities-Prohibition-Act-2018.pdf; https://www.idea.int/news/rolac-informs-relevant-stakeholders-implementation-2023-pwd-accessibility-regulations), and NITDA's *Standards and Guidelines for Government Websites* (v3.2.1), whose §5.3.3.1 tells agencies to "adopt the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) developed by W3C" — guidance for government sites, pre-dating WCAG 2.2, and not law for a campaign: https://www.nitda.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/StandardsGuidelinesforGovtWebsitesV321.pdf. The NCC Consumer Code of Practice Regulations 2024 impose accessibility duties on complaint channels and service for elderly and disabled persons, not on websites: https://consumer.ncc.gov.ng/articles/104-highlights-of-the-ncc-consumer-code-of-practice-regulations-2024. The African Disability Protocol entered into force in 2024 per the AU treaty record (date discrepancy noted in §7).

**Conclusion drawn.** WCAG 2.2 is cited as the *normative benchmark* this product volunteers to, and the CRPD/ADP/2023 Regulations framing is used for obligations context. Any claim of statutory compliance would be wrong in the other direction — nobody will sue this site under a web-accessibility statute that does not exist — but a campaign that says "fully accessible" without an audit is making a claim it cannot evidence, which is the failure mode this whole product is built to avoid.

**Scholarly support for the design instinct.** Fogg, Tseng and Hernandez's CHI 2001 study of 4,632 respondents found that roughly 46% of credibility judgements began with look-and-feel and design before content: "What Makes Web Sites Credible?", https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i290-10/f05/bjfogg.pdf. Selejan et al. (2016) record credibility signals forming within ~500 ms of exposure: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4863498/. This is the empirical case for the mobile-and-calm-colour brief the client gave in plain language: on a shared screenshot, the visual impression *is* the argument. Full texts of the first were read at the summary level only; the numbers quoted are from the abstract and the paper's own reporting.

### 3.2 Performance

Core Web Vitals thresholds, at p75 of field data: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1, with a page passing when ≥75% of views meet all three — https://web.dev/articles/vitals and https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds. INP replaced FID on 12 March 2024: https://web.dev/blog/inp-cwv-march-12. The PRD's NFR-8 targets are these numbers plus a TTFB budget set by the project (p75 < 600 ms cached / < 1.2 s uncached), which is an internal target, not a published threshold, and is labelled as such wherever it appears.

### 3.3 Data protection

**Nigeria.** The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 received presidential assent in June 2023 (assent-date conflict in §7) and establishes the Nigeria Data Protection Commission in s.4. Official text: https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf (scanned copy — section wording should be re-verified against the Gazette). The operative detail for this product:

- **s.30 lists political opinions as sensitive personal data.** This is the most important sentence in the entire research record for a political campaign site, and it changes the meaning of every form on this product. A volunteer's name and phone number is not merely personal data; the fact of submitting it to a named candidate's site is itself an expression of political opinion.
- Lawful bases are in **s.25(1)** (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public interest, legitimate interests); general principles in s.24; **consent burden, "silence does not constitute consent", and withdrawal in s.26**; privacy notice in s.27; DPIA in s.28; data-subject rights — confirmation/access, copy, rectification, **erasure**, restriction — in s.34; objection in s.36; automated decision-making in s.37.
- The **NDPA General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID), issued 20 March 2025**, is the instrument that operationalises it: https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf.
- Practical commentary: https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG (17 Mar 2026) and https://www.legal500.com/en/intelligence/nigeria/privacy/important-data-protection-obligations-and-practices-for-companies-in-nigeria.

**Enforcement is not theoretical.** The NDPC fined Fidelity Bank ₦555.8m (22 Aug 2024, https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/nigerian-data-agency-fines-fidelity-bank-breaches-2024-08-22/) and MultiChoice Nigeria ₦766m (7 Jul 2025, https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/nigerian-agency-fines-multichoice-766-million-naira-data-privacy-breaches-2025-07-07/), issued compliance notices and published a 1,368-organisation defaulter list in Aug 2025 (https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/privacy/the-ndpc-compliance-notice-a-new-era-of-accountability-for-data-controllers-and-processors-in-nigeria; https://www.africanlawbusiness.com/news/nigeria-launches-widespread-probe-into-data-protection-violations/). Its ₦32.8m-equivalent action against Meta over unlawful processing of Nigerians' data was settled/annulled by Oct 2025 (https://www.africanews.com/2025-10-07/meta-agrees-to-328-million-data-privacy-settlement-with-nigeria/), which makes the *scraping-based complaint* a live template rather than a closed precedent.

**Design consequences that follow from the statute rather than from taste.** Storing `volunteers.consent_at` is not decoration — it is what lets the campaign answer a withdrawal or erasure request with a date. `toPublic()` stripping reporter phone numbers (ADR-025) is minimisation with a hard penalty attached. The requirement that a Help Desk report not be a publication is both a reputational rule and a data-processing rule. Spanish regulators have fined parties (PP and Podemos) over voter scraping and the publication of officials' personal data — https://www.newtral.es/multas-partidos-politicos-proteccion-datos/20210806/ — which is the closest available signal of what an African-equivalent regulator might later do with a campaign's supporter list.

**Europe, because the site is readable from anywhere.** GDPR is Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (adopted 27 Apr 2016, applicable 25 May 2018; https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). Political opinions are Art. 9(1) special-category data, and **Recital 56 expressly permits parties to compile political-opinion data in electoral activities for reasons of public interest, subject to suitable safeguards**: https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-56/. The UK mirrors Art. 6/9. This matters to a non-obvious extent: a diaspora supporter in Berlin filling in `/join` puts the campaign inside a jurisdiction where a foundation for the exception exists but the safeguards are mandatory. The product does not claim GDPR compliance; it notes the exposure in `10-SECURITY-AND-COMPLIANCE.md`.

### 3.4 Human-in-the-loop and synthetic media

**EU AI Act** — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, adopted 13 June 2024, OJ 12 July 2024, in force 1 August 2024. **Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on 2 August 2026**: 50(2) machine-readable marking of synthetic content and 50(4) disclosure of "deepfakes" — AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video resembling real persons, objects, places or events — with an artistic-work exception and **no campaign-specific carve-out**. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng and https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/. The Commission's guidelines on Art. 50 and the **Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content, adopted 10 June 2026**, are the operational reading: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations, https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content.

Nigeria is not bound by the AI Act. The relevance is threefold: it is the clearest existing articulation of why "no AI-generated portrait of a real candidate" is a professional standard rather than a quirk; any European-facing use of the material is in scope; and a campaign that later expands nationally will meet equivalent expectations. Meanwhile the local evidence says the risk is current, not hypothetical (§4.3).

**Governance frameworks cited as method, not as compliance targets:** NIST AI RMF 1.0 (AI 100-1), 26 Jan 2023 — https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf; the Generative AI Profile NIST AI 600-1, 11 Jul 2024 — https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf; ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI management systems), published 18 Dec 2023 — https://www.iso.org/standard/42001.html.

**The academic limit that justifies ADR-013.** Gillespie and Pomerantz, "Content moderation, AI, and the question of scale", *Social Media + Society* 6(2), 2020, DOI 10.1177/2053951720943234 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2053951720943234 — argues automation is a compromise for scale, not a solution to context. The Oversight Board's election-year review reached the compatible operational conclusion: prioritise faster escalation *to human review* — https://www.oversightboard.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Oversight-Board-Elections-Paper-May-2024FINAL.pdf. A five-person desk does not have a scale problem, so it should not buy an automation compromise. That is the whole of the argument against a generative layer in the editing surface, and it is a citation, not a preference.

### 3.5 Caching and self-hosting literature

The Next.js App Router docs (last updated 15 Oct 2026) define ISR semantics, `revalidate`, `revalidatePath`/`revalidateTag`, and the `x-nextjs-cache` HIT/STALE/MISS/REVALIDATED states: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/incremental-static-regeneration. History: ISR arrived in v9.5, on-demand revalidation in v12.2, App Router in v13, stable `cacheHandler` in v14.1.

The single most consequential sentence for this deployment, quoted as published in the CDN-caching guide (last updated 25 Aug 2026): CDN-level caching alone does not support on-demand revalidation — `revalidatePath`/`revalidateTag` invalidate the Next.js server cache, "but the CDN will continue serving its cached copy until the `s-maxage` TTL expires"; the purge has to be triggered alongside the revalidation call, for both HTML and RSC variants. https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/cdn-caching. The self-hosting guide adds that the file cache is per-instance, so revalidation "only invalidates the instance that receives the call" — which is why a multi-instance container deployment without a shared cache has a staleness problem no amount of code fixes. https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/self-hosting

HTTP caching itself (RFC 9111, "HTTP Caching", STD 98, June 2022, obsoleting RFC 7234) defines freshness, `max-age`, `s-maxage` and `stale-while-revalidate` — and pointedly does not define an invalidation API; purging is always a vendor extension: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111. Vercel's own framing of the TTL-versus-purge trade-off: https://vercel.com/docs/caching/cache-control-headers (last updated 14 Sep 2026).

**Conclusion drawn for this project:** the ISR window (300/900/3600 s) is an *editorial staleness budget*, and behind a CDN it is a worst-case bound rather than a guarantee. A Truth Room that lags an hour during a live rumour is a political problem. That reframing is `DEPLOYMENT-GUIDE.md` §6.4 and open question 2 in the PRD.

### 3.6 Platform political-advertising rules

Meta requires political and social-issue advertisers to verify identity and include a "paid for by"-style disclaimer, and requires disclosure where such ads use AI to create or alter photorealistic images, video, or realistic audio — announced 28 Nov 2023, in force from Jan 2024, reiterated in the Dec 2024 elections recap, with organic AI labelling from May 2024. https://about.fb.com/news/2023/11/how-meta-is-planning-for-elections-in-2024/ · https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/ · https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP/ · https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1010479435004531. **The ad-standards page indicates automated AI-detection labelling of ad media beginning 1 June 2026; that date could not be confirmed from an automated fetch and must be re-read in a browser before it is relied on [unverified].**

Meta's "Coordinated/Inauthentic Behaviour" standard, introduced as an enforcement category on 6 December 2018, prohibits misrepresenting ownership or control of accounts and coordinated inauthentic activity: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior/ · https://about.fb.com/news/2018/12/inside-feed-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior/. The US FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials took effect 21 Oct 2024 and bans fake or undisclosed-incentive testimonials: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers.

**Why this is in the discovery record for a website:** the platform rules describe the environment the site's shares travel through, and they define the professional norm the product is aligned to. A campaign that astroturfs risks the account network; a campaign that publishes an undisclosed AI image risks the ad rejection and the story. The prohibitions in PRD §12 are therefore both ethics and distribution strategy.

## 4. Domain research — what actually happens to campaigns

Three of these events happened to the infrastructure of a campaign or an electoral body. That is the category this product is in, and the list is why the risk table in the PRD reads the way it does.

### 4.1 Nigerian online misinformation with documented real-world effect

- **25–28 Feb 2023 — INEC's IReV results portal outage.** The portal was unavailable for hours on election day and a wave of old videos and false "INEC was hacked / results already declared" claims followed; INEC denied a breach. https://www.bbc.com/news/world-africa-64797274 · https://brandefense.io/knowledge-hub/incidents/inec-irev-cyberattack-claims-2023/ **Directly relevant:** an unavailable authoritative system is the precondition for a rumour, and the site's availability posture (NFR-11) exists because of this pattern.

- **9 Apr 2023 — the "Russian boy hacked INEC" story,** debunked by Africa Check. https://www.legit.ng/politics/1528385-russian-boy-hacked-inecs-website-released-authentic-2023-election-results-fact-emerges/

- **15–19 Aug 2026 — Osun governorship poll:** fake "town-hall result" graphics and duplicated result sheets on IReV were fact-checked. https://guardian.ng/news/osun-poll-same-result-sheets-uploaded-multiple-times-on-irev-paaca-alleges/ · https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/902500-analysis-osundecides2026-whats-moving-in-the-digital-information-space.html **Most recent and most instructive for a 2027 state-level contest: a fabricated graphic is a design problem, and the defence is a citable record.**
- **3 Oct 2026 — INEC disowned a fake "online voter registration open Sep 28–Oct 27" site** and urged Nigerians not to submit personal data to it; Africa Check republished the debunk on 13 Oct. https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/inec-warns-nigerians-against-fake-voter-registration-website/ · https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/meta-programme-fact-checks/ignore-posts-claiming-nigerias-electoral-body-announced **A campaign site can be impersonated within days of launching; the answer is a canonical URL and a public channel list, which this product has in the Site Copy tab.**

- **27–28 Oct 2026 — a cyberattack forced the US FEC to take its eFiling platform offline roughly 41 hours before midterms,** blocking mandatory 24/48-hour ad-spend disclosures. https://www.reuters.com/technology/hacker-trying-breach-us-federal-election-commission-system-email-shows-2026-10-27/ · https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/fec-takes-down-electronic-filing-platform-after-attempted-hack/416866/ **The incumbent authority's system went down during the window that needed it most.**

### 4.3 Synthetic media against candidates, and the responses

- **29 Oct–1 Nov 2023 — Slovakia:** AI audio of pro-EU candidate Šimečka apparently discussing vote rigging surfaced two days before the vote; police opened an investigation, later continued a third time. https://cedmohub.eu/how-ai-generated-content-influenced-parliamentary-elections-in-slovakia-the-slovak-police-will-investigate-the-recording-for-a-third-time/
- **8 Feb 2024 — the FCC ruled AI-cloned voices in robocalls illegal** after the fake-Biden New Hampshire call; in May 2024 it proposed $6m against the operative and $1m against the routing carrier. https://www.npr.org/2024-05-23/nx-s1-4977582/fcc-ai-deepfake-robocall-biden-new-hampshire-political-operative · https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-settles-spoofed-ai-generated-robocalls-case
- **DUBAWA, published Jan 2026 covering 2025 — six AI deepfakes that deceived Nigerians,** including Kanu "prison" videos, a Gumi bandit-welfare clip, and a fabricated Tinubu–Trump clash. https://dubawa.org/six-ai-generated-deepfakes-that-deceived-nigerians-in-2025/
- **5 Jun 2026 — the Nigerian Presidency confirmed the arrest of a suspect** over an AI-cloned Tinubu "State of the Nation" audio and threatened action over a circulated variant. https://www.bbc.com/pidgin/articles/ckgpged5k4lo **A sitting head of state's cloned voice, prosecuted. This is the local norm-setting event, and it is 5 months old.**

**What the product does with this.** No AI imagery of the candidate (constraint 1), the `exact` frame rule, credits that name the outlet, and Truth Room rulings that describe claims the campaign has actually met. Note the asymmetry the events reveal and the design accepts: the site cannot stop a deepfake being made of its candidate. It can make its own record so well-sourced that a correction has somewhere to live — and can make it so visibly well-sourced that the correction is credible.

- Empirically: Weber, Johnson, Starbird & Aral (2021), "Amplifying influence through coordinated behaviour in social networks", *Nature Human Behaviour* — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34745379/; Keller & Koltay (2017), "How to Manipulate Social Media: Analyzing Political Astroturfing Using Ground Truth Data from South Korea", ICWSM — https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14941.

**Two conclusions.** First, the platform's enforcement is real, recent, and targeted at West Africa specifically — an astroturfing capability would be an account-loss risk, not an advantage. Second, and more uncomfortable for the product's assumptions: **the third-party fact-checking layer that a campaign could previously enlist against a rumour was largely dismantled in Jan 2025.** A campaign's own citable record moved from "nice" to "primary defence", which is the strongest external justification this research produced for the Truth Room and `/impact#sources`.

## 8. What this research changes about the product

Recorded as findings, because a discovery document that concludes "we built what we planned" did not discover anything.

1. **Do the accessibility item the standard names and the build missed.** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) requires an explicit control for auto-updating content. The carousel and the stat strip need a visible pause, not only `prefers-reduced-motion`. This is an A, not an AA, and it is the cheapest real gap to close.
2. **Add a timeout to the volunteer route.** §6's last row. Small, and it is the difference between a slow network and a lost lead.
3. **Read "Manufactured Doubt" (Aug 2026) before the next requirement round.** A Nigerian pre-election threat assessment covering 2027 AI and misinformation patterns, produced by Brain Builders with FactCheckHub, is the closest available spec for what this site will be attacked by. It was not in the input set.
4. **Consider a prebunking surface.** The inoculation literature (§4.5) reports measurable effects from teaching people how manipulation works *before* they meet it. A plain-language page — "how we verify, how a fake clip is made, what we will never post" — is content, costs nothing architecturally, and converts the site from reactive to partially preventive. It also makes the "no AI imagery" constraint legible to voters instead of invisible.
5. **Treat staleness as an editorial number.** The CDN-caching literature (§3.5) means the ISR window is a worst-case bound. HQ should set the budget per page type; the technical answer already exists.
6. **The absence of a legal accessibility duty is not a licence.** It is the reason the site should claim "built to WCAG 2.2 as far as we have audited, here is what is not yet audited" rather than "accessible" — a claim a hostile reader can test in ninety seconds.
7. **Impersonation is a named, current risk in Nigeria** (fake INEC registration site, Oct 2026). The canonical-URL discipline and confirmed-channel list already in the CMS should be paired with a published statement of what the campaign will never ask for — money, BVN, voter-card details — which is the cheapest anti-impersonation control available to a site this size.
8. **Donations stay out.** §4.2's ~270 fake campaign sites harvesting donor data in one US state, three months ago, is the evidence, and it converts a caution into a decision.
